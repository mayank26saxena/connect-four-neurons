Neuron atlas · Connect Four · draft

Neurons an agent described, checked on games it never saw

An AI agent ran experiments on 120 neurons of a small Connect Four transformer and wrote down what each one detects, in English and as a rule over the board. We scored every rule on held-out games, next to 40 random directions the agent was not told were random.

Draft, not publishedStudies (q) and (r), pre-registered in docs/PLAN.md

01How each description was checked

Following Goodfire's "Neuron Scientist" (Tigges et al. 2026), a describing agent looked at positions where a unit fires, tested hypotheses by playing out its own positions, steered the unit, and could ask a scoring tool for feedback up to eight times. Its final answer was frozen, then checked on validation games it never had access to.

On each cardWhat it measures
Held-out F1How well the agent's rule predicts when the unit fires, on unseen games (0 to 1). The unit "fires" when it is beyond its own top 3% of activations.
DictionaryThe best of our 659 hand-written concepts for the same unit, chosen the same way. The agent should beat it. The concept list numbers columns and rows from 1; the agents' descriptions number them from 0.
JudgeA fresh model saw only the English description and 20 pairs of boards, one firing and one not, and had to pick the firing one. Chance is 50%.
SimulationReplace the unit's activation with the level the rule predicts and measure how much of the model's behaviour comes back, as a share of what a perfect 10-level reader recovers (it can exceed 100% when the rule's levels happen to suit the model better).
SynthesisWhether a position the agent built drove the unit above its strongest value in 5,000 recorded games.
ConfidenceThe agent's own rating, given before scoring. "Low" means it found no clean concept.

Example boards are drawn from the player about to move: their stones, the opponent's, a ring marks the last stone played. For real neurons they are the four strongest firings in the recorded games, with the pole's activation; for random directions, the positions the agent built. Columns are numbered 0 to 6 from the left in the descriptions.

03What held, and what did not

Both studies were pre-registered with the same tests. Study (r) repeated (q) after removing an artefact: every game starts from the same few positions, so any direction that happens to be extreme on, say, the empty board "fires" there and gets an exact one-line rule. (r) excluded positions with fewer than four stones and used 30 random directions instead of 10.

04How to read this atlas

  • A good description shows that a unit tracks something, not that the neuron basis is special: several random directions were described about as well, and in (r) real neurons beat random directions by less than the pre-registered margin.
  • Rules describe when a unit fires much better than how much: the simulation share is low for most units, so these are detectors, not full accounts of what the neuron does.
  • The 0.5 bar for "well described" was set after the studies finished, for this atlas, and is not a pre-registered threshold. The opening-unit label is a post-hoc check with a fixed rule; no score was changed.
  • Four (r) units used local analysis close to the protocol's limits, and five judges answered 19 of 20 pairs; excluding them changes no verdict (docs/NS_RESULTS.md).
